# Film Editing Masterclass

*Source: Walter Murch & Greg Keast - Film Editing Masterclass*

Editing is the bold act of letting go of good material so the core truth can breathe. A masterclass overview of film language demonstrates how precise cuts structure the human experience.

## The Philosophy of the Cut

**Kill your darlings:** Delete scenes, lines, or shots you love if they stall the narrative momentum.

**Trust the gap:** Leave empty space so the audience can feel the emotion and build their own connections.

**Shape the rhythm:** Pace the work by deciding what the viewer needs to experience at that exact second.

## What Stays and What Goes

**Keep the core:** Retain only the beats that drive the emotional or informational truth forward.

**Remove the friction:** Cut the redundant explanations, lingering introductions, and polite transitions that weaken the punch.

## For future Claude

This note captures the core philosophy of film editing from Walter Murch and Greg Keast's masterclass. It frames editing as subtraction in service of truth - killing darlings, trusting gaps, shaping rhythm, keeping the core, and removing friction. These principles transfer beyond film to any craft involving narrative, structure, or communication.
